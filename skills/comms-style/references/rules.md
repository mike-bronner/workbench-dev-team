# ASD-STE100 rule-by-rule, restated as writing guidance

Verified against the official Issue 9 PDF (January 2025, [asd-ste100.org](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)) — not a paraphrase. The document is 434 pages: Part 1 (Writing rules, all 9 sections, all 53 rules, plus a Grammar Reference appendix) is ~85 pages; Part 2 (the dictionary) is the other ~306 pages.

Every rule below is restated in original wording — never ASD's copyrighted text. There is no checking tool behind this document; it exists so the principles in `SKILL.md` are traceable back to something real, and so you can go deeper than the summary when a specific case is unclear. Apply these as you write — none of them need a perfect, mechanical pass.

## Part 1, Section 1 — Words (14 rules)

| Rule | Restated | How it applies here |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Use only dictionary-approved words. | Applies — use plain, precise words. No dictionary to check against; use judgment. |
| 1.2 | An approved word may be used only as its specified part of speech. | Applies loosely — don't let a noun drift into verb use mid-comment ("let's issue this" vs. "the issue"). Judgment call, not a rule to verify. |
| 1.3 | An approved word may be used only with its approved meaning. | Applies loosely — pick one sense of a word and stick to it within a comment. |
| 1.4 | Use only the approved verb/adjective forms given in the dictionary. | Folds into the verb-form guidance below (simple tense, active voice). |
| 1.5 | You may use a word that fits a technical-noun category. | Guidance for building a domain dictionary — not applicable; these agents aren't curating a controlled vocabulary. |
| 1.6 | An unapproved word is allowed only as (part of) a technical noun. | Same as 1.5 — not applicable. |
| 1.7 | Do not use a technical noun as a verb. | Applies loosely — don't verb a noun ("let's PR this," "table it" as a verb) when a real verb exists. |
| 1.8 | Use technical nouns approved in your company/industry/field. | Not applicable — dictionary-construction guidance. |
| 1.9 | Prefer a short, easy technical noun when you must choose one. | Applies as general advice — prefer the shorter, plainer term. |
| 1.10 | Do not use regional, slang, or jargon words as technical nouns. | Applies loosely — avoid slang; this repo's own domain terms (item/issue/PR/AC) are the exception, they're precise here, not slang. |
| 1.11 | Do not use different technical nouns for the same item. | **Core principle** — one name per referent (SKILL.md, "Vocabulary"). |
| 1.12 | You may use a word that fits a technical-verb category. | Not applicable — dictionary-construction guidance. |
| 1.13 | (Technical-verb category example/sub-rule.) | Not applicable — same reasoning. |
| 1.14 | Use American English spelling. | **Core principle** — American spelling (SKILL.md, "Vocabulary"). |

## Part 1, Section 2 — Multi-word nouns (2 rules)

| Rule | Restated | How it applies here |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | Do not string together more than 3 nouns. | **Core principle** — no more than three nouns in a row (SKILL.md, "Sentences"). |
| 2.2 | When a technical noun needs more than 3 words, use hyphens or another method to combine it. | The fix for 2.1's finding — hyphenate or rephrase. |

## Part 1, Section 3 — Verbs (7 rules)

| Rule | Restated | How it applies here |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Use only the verb forms the dictionary gives for that verb. | Applies loosely — no dictionary to check against; use the simple forms below instead. |
| 3.2 | Use only these forms/tenses: infinitive, imperative, simple present/past/future. | **Core principle** — simple tense (SKILL.md, "Verbs"). |
| 3.3 | A past participle may be used only as an adjective. | Applies loosely — judgment call, minor. |
| 3.4 | Do not use auxiliary verbs for complex verb constructions (e.g. perfect tense). | **Core principle** — avoid perfect tense (SKILL.md, "Verbs"). |
| 3.5 | Use the "-ing" form only as a technical noun or noun modifier, never as a main verb. | **Core principle** — avoid "-ing" as the main verb (SKILL.md, "Verbs"). |
| 3.6 | Use active voice; passive only if the agent is unknown. | **Core principle** — active voice (SKILL.md, "Verbs"). |
| 3.7 | Use an approved verb to describe an action, not a noun. | **Core principle** — don't freeze a verb into a noun (SKILL.md, "Vocabulary"). |

## Part 1, Section 4 — Sentences (5 rules)

| Rule | Restated | How it applies here |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | Write short, clear sentences. | **Core principle** — short, direct sentences (SKILL.md, "Sentences"). |
| 4.2 | Do not omit words or use contractions. | **Core principle**, the contraction half (SKILL.md, "Sentences"). Omitted words: judgment call. |
| 4.3 | Use a vertical list for complex, multi-item text. | **Core principle** — prefer a list over a comma-stuffed sentence (SKILL.md, "Sentences"). |
| 4.4 | Use connecting words/phrases to link related sentences. | Applies as general advice — use "because," "so," "therefore" to show how sentences relate, when it helps. |
| 4.5 | Use an article or demonstrative before a noun/multi-word noun where applicable. | Applies loosely — natural English does this already; not a special watch-item. |

## Part 1, Section 5 — Procedural writing (5 rules)

| Rule | Restated | How it applies here |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | Max 20 words per sentence in procedures. | **Core principle** — procedural register (SKILL.md, "Two registers"). |
| 5.2 | One instruction per sentence, unless the actions are simultaneous. | **Core principle** — one instruction per sentence (SKILL.md, "Sentences"). |
| 5.3 | Write instructions in the imperative (command) form. | **Core principle** — commands, not statements about the reader (SKILL.md, "Verbs"). |
| 5.4 | State a condition the reader needs first, before the instruction. | **Core principle** — condition before instruction (SKILL.md, "Sentences"). |
| 5.5 | Notes give information only, never instructions. | Applies when you label something a note — keep it informational. |

## Part 1, Section 6 — Descriptive writing (6 rules)

| Rule | Restated | How it applies here |
|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | Give information gradually; one topic per sentence. | **Core principle** — one topic per sentence (SKILL.md, "Sentences"), descriptive register. |
| 6.2 | Use key words/phrases to give the text a logical structure. | Applies as general advice — repeat key terms deliberately rather than re-describing the same idea in new words each time. |
| 6.3 | Max 25 words per sentence in descriptive text. | **Core principle** — descriptive register (SKILL.md, "Two registers"). |
| 6.4 | Use paragraphs to group related information. | **Core principle** — group related sentences into paragraphs (SKILL.md, "Paragraphs"). |
| 6.5 | One topic per paragraph. | Applies as a judgment call — same idea as 6.1, one level up. |
| 6.6 | No paragraph longer than 6 sentences. | **Core principle** — paragraph length (SKILL.md, "Paragraphs"). |

## Part 1, Section 8 — Punctuation and word count (7 rules)

| Rule | Restated | How it applies here |
|---|---|---|
| 8.1 | All standard punctuation is allowed except the semicolon. | **Core principle** — no semicolons (SKILL.md, "Sentences"). |
| 8.2 | Use hyphens to connect directly-related words. | Pairs with 2.2 — how to fix an overlong noun phrase. |
| 8.3 | Parentheses are for specific uses (illustration/text references, etc). | Not meaningfully applicable — this domain doesn't have illustration cross-references. |

## The dictionary (Part 2, ~306 pages)

ASD's ~900 approved + ~1,200 unapproved-with-alternatives words are copyrighted and not reproduced here. The vocabulary principle above (one word per concept, no synonyms, plain verbs over nominalizations, no marketing adjectives, no phrasal verbs) is the *shape* of what the real dictionary enforces, applied by judgment rather than by looking up ~2,100 entries.
